## Average-quantity method and owner direction

Standard 1.1.1-6(2)-(3) permits a producer or manufacturer to calculate an
average quantity from actual or average nutrient quantities in the food's
ingredients, using the method considered best to represent the average and
accounting for lot-to-lot variation. This provides a Code-supported
ingredient-based calculation method; it does not mean that every NIP must
ignore processing or that the Code universally requires a raw/as-added
display basis.

On 2026-11-19, the owner adopted ingoing recipe nutrients and quantities as
the general calculation method for ordinary NIP cases, where this method best
represents average quantities. The owner closed the planned UXR14-UXR15
automatic retention-factor research and label-workflow expansion. Existing
optional manual-retention and direct finished-food routes remain unchanged
under [ADR 018](./adr/018-nutrient-retention.md). This product decision does
not add a factor or claim that all processing changes are immaterial.

Specific food-state provisions continue to apply when triggered: Standard
1.2.8-11 for food to be reconstituted with water, -12 for food directed to be
drained, and -13 for food intended to be prepared or consumed with another
food. The combined-food column is permitted generally under -13(2) and
required in the conditions in -13(3)-(5). The [Food Act 1984 (Vic), version
119](https://www.legislation.vic.gov.au/in-force/acts/food-act-1984/119),
applies the Code in Victoria. These provisions do not prescribe a universal
cooking-retention factor.

Standard 1.2.8-11 to -13 are triggered by what the label or labelling
indicates about consumer use. Cooking or other processing during manufacture
does not, by itself, trigger those provisions or establish a universal need
for an additional prepared-food column. The actual composition of the food
still matters to its nutrition information.

Applicability must be assessed separately. For example, Standard 1.2.8-3
excludes infant formula products from that Standard, and 1.2.8-5 provides
specific NIP exceptions and conditions. Do not infer that every product
with preparation or draining directions necessarily requires a NIP.
The N2 source check also found that food for infants need not comply with
1.2.8-11: Standard 2.9.2-11(1)(c). For dehydrated or concentrated food for
infants, 2.9.2-12 instead requires reconstitution directions and NIP
particulars expressed as reconstituted, with a specific rule where more
than one preparing fluid is nominated. Do not treat infant formula products
as food for infants under Standard 2.9.2. The infant-food transition and
other category-specific requirements need separate assessment.

## Calculation and workflow implications

These are conservative product requirements derived from the verified
provisions and the project's missing-data policy, not additional statutory
formulae:

- Keep manufacturing state, food as sold and consumer preparation directions
  distinct. Support more than one basis where relevant; do not force every
  product into a single universal state or extra column.
- Determine whether a trigger applies from recorded label directions and
  product facts where reliable. Ask only for information not already known;
  unclear or conflicting directions require review.
- For reconstitution, use the relevant directions and supported preparation
  amounts and resulting composition. Do not invent a water ratio or yield.
- For draining, use evidence appropriate to the drained food and its
  composition. Whole-pack composition or drained weight alone does not
  establish how much of each nutrient remains after draining.
- For preparation or consumption with another food, retain the identity,
  amount and nutrient evidence of that food and distinguish the combined
  result from the original food. Apply FS-05 only when its claim condition is
  established.
- Where a special food-state basis applies, or where the chosen calculation
  method needs to account for processing, yield, added or lost material and
  nutrient changes need adequate evidence. A measured final weight alone
  does not prove that nutrients or additives were retained in a particular
  proportion.
- Preserve the source, relevant directions, food state, amounts and
  calculation path. If applicability, yield, composition or another needed
  input cannot be established, return **REVIEW REQUIRED** rather than an
  invented value or a PASS. Existing as-sold draft behaviour and release
  gates are unchanged by this research.

The provisions above do not prescribe a nutrient-retention factor or a
general calculation method for losses and gains during cooking,
reconstitution or draining. This is a finding about the provisions checked,
not a claim that no other law or method applies. A particular calculation
method and its evidence requirements need separate validation.

## Cases for later implementation and tests

All examples are fictional; applicability of a NIP is assumed only where
stated.

| Case | Expected handling |
| --- | --- |
| Powder label directs reconstitution with a stated amount of water; a NIP is required. | Apply FS-02. Calculate only with enough supported preparation and composition evidence; missing or contradictory directions or amounts require review. |
| Label directs draining; a NIP is required. | Apply FS-03 and identify the drained basis. A drained weight alone, or only an undrained nutrient profile, is insufficient to establish all drained nutrient values. |
| Cereal labelling indicates consumption with milk; a NIP is required. | FS-04 permits the combined column. If a claim requiring nutrition information is made about the cereal, FS-05 requires it and the as-prepared serving size. Missing milk amount or composition prevents an evidenced combined calculation. |
| Food is cooked during manufacture and sold without directions to reconstitute, drain or consume with another food. | Do not trigger FS-02 to FS-05 solely because it was cooked. Evaluate NIP applicability and finished-food composition separately. |
| Labelling gives vague or conflicting preparation instructions. | Do not infer the trigger from the product type. Retain the wording and require review if applicability or inputs cannot be resolved. |
| A product qualifies for a relevant NIP exception. | Do not automatically require a full NIP simply because a preparation direction exists; evaluate the exception and applicable claim conditions separately. |

Tests should also cover solid/semi-solid versus liquid unit quantities,
both claim and no-claim combined-food cases, and cases where ingredients,
preparation amounts or composition evidence are missing.

## Unresolved and outside this check

- The interaction and presentation when multiple provisions apply to the
  same instructions, such as reconstitution followed by draining or use
  with another food, needs interpretation and potentially reviewer input.
  Do not silently suppress one trigger.
- Whether a particular direction satisfies a trigger, and whether evidence
  represents the relevant food state, depends on the actual facts.
- The relationship between water reconstitution and the "other food"
  condition in 1.2.8-13 remains **TO BE VERIFIED** for overlapping cases.
- Full product-specific exemptions, state-specific law, claim rules, serving
  selection, evidence hierarchy, calculation methods and every transition
  outside the cited provisions were not verified in N1. Nutrient catalogue
  research remains N2; no regulatory facts were added to application code.
